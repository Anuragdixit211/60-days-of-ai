Data sources & methodology

Where our nutritional data comes from

NutriScope is an educational tool. Nutritional values are approximate averages from published databases and may vary by brand, preparation method, and region.

Nutritional databases

📗
ICMR–NIN Food Composition Tables 2017
Primary reference for Indian foods (rice, roti, dal, paneer etc.). Published by the National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad, India.
🇺🇸
USDA FoodData Central (FDC)
Used for micronutrient data on fruits, vegetables, dairy and meats. Freely available at fdc.nal.usda.gov.
🌐
WHO / FAO Nutrient Requirements
Recommended daily intakes for vitamins, minerals and macronutrients used to set targets.

Calculation methodology

🧮
Mifflin-St Jeor equation (BMR)
BMR (male) = 10W + 6.25H − 5A + 5  |  BMR (female) = 10W + 6.25H − 5A − 161. Multiplied by activity factor for TDEE.
🎯
Macro split
Protein: 1.2 g/kg body weight · Fat: 25% of TDEE ÷ 9 · Carbs: remainder. Adjusted ±500 kcal for weight loss / gain goals.
📏
Micronutrient targets
Based on ICMR 2010 RDA for Indians, adjusted for gender, age group, and special conditions (anaemia, pregnancy, etc.).

Limitations & disclaimer

  • Values represent average cooked/raw weights unless specified.
  • Home-cooked food nutrient content varies significantly with preparation method, water used, and oil added.
  • Portion estimates are approximations. Use a kitchen scale for accuracy.
  • NutriScope does not store any data on servers — everything is in your browser only.
  • Do not use this tool to manage medical conditions without professional supervision.
